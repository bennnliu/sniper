Development data, in-sample, 7,680 trades on 752 high-volatility days. AUC measures how well one feature alone ranks winners above losers: 0.50 is a coin, 1.00 is perfect. Method, definitions and what is measured, simulated or assumed
  • Label: a trade is a winner when its result after costs is above zero. Trades of the nine settings on one day are near-copies, so every interval and t-statistic resamples whole days.
  • Inputs: 33 numbers known at the decision minute (band distance, VWAP distance, volume, time of day, overnight and previous-day moves, ES confirmation, what the other settings are doing) and, for the convolutional network, the last 60 one-minute price changes.
  • Models: logistic regression; gradient-boosted trees; a network with three hidden layers; a network with two convolution layers over the price path joined to the same table. Architectures were fixed in advance and not tuned. Each network is the average of three random starts.
  • Walk-forward: for each test year the model sees only earlier years. The last 15% of the training days are held back to stop training and to set the skip threshold at the 33rd percentile of scores.
  • Skipping a trade means not taking that setting's position from its entry to its exit. The rule's own bookkeeping is unchanged.
  • Measured: trade entries, exits and results on 1-minute bars. Assumed: 1.11 points per round trip per MNQ. Simulated: all Monte Carlo figures.
  • Trials: these four models bring the project's count to 195.
